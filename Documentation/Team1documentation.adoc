=== 1.2 Current Situation, Needs, Ideas

=== 1.2.1 Current Situation
For native Spanish speakers, learning and pronouncing English words and phrases presents significant challenges due to fundamental differences in sound production and auditory perception between the languages. Learners struggle to distinguish and reproduce unfamiliar English phonemes like /θ/ (as in "think") and /ð/ (as in "this"), which don't exist in Spanish. These pronunciation difficulties lead to communication breakdowns in professional and social contexts, causing frustration and limiting opportunities.

Mobile learning applications often exacerbate these challenges through technical limitations: processing delays during authentication and audio operations disrupt learning flow, while generic loading indicators waste valuable practice time. Users report abandoning practice sessions due to these technical frustrations rather than learning difficulties.

=== 1.2.2 Needs
* **Need for immediate, understandable feedback**: Learners require specific guidance on why their pronunciation differs from native speakers, not just correctness indicators
* **Need for consistent practice opportunities**: Users need accessible practice that fits into busy schedules without technical barriers
* **Need for progress visibility**: Clear indicators of improvement help maintain motivation through challenging learning periods
* **Need for inclusive learning experiences**: The application must accommodate diverse abilities including visual, auditory, and motor limitations
* **Need for reliable technical performance**: Consistent application behavior builds trust and encourages regular use
* **Need for engaging waiting experiences**: Processing delays should provide educational value rather than frustration

=== 1.2.3 Ideas
* Interactive pronunciation exercises with visual articulation guides showing tongue and lip placement
* Phoneme-focused practice modules targeting specific Spanish-to-English sound challenges
* Progress tracking that highlights incremental improvements in difficult sound categories
* Adaptive learning paths that adjust difficulty based on individual pronunciation patterns
* Educational loading states that teach pronunciation facts during processing delays
* Accessibility-first design ensuring all learners can benefit from pronunciation practice
* Robust account management preventing common registration issues and ensuring data security

=== 1.3 Scope, Span, and Synopsis

=== 1.3.1 Scope and Span
*Scope*:: Mobile-based language learning application specializing in pronunciation improvement for Spanish speakers. The project focuses on building an accessible, reliable foundation for pronunciation practice with particular attention to technical robustness and inclusive design.

=== 1.9 Risk Analysis and Mitigation Strategy

==== 1.9.1 Risk Identification and Assessment Cycle

The project conducted a comprehensive risk analysis following the identification-assessment-countermeasure-evaluation cycle:

*High Priority Risks Identified*:
- **Authentication Race Conditions**: Async operations causing state inconsistencies
- **Accessibility Compliance Gaps**: Potential exclusion of users with disabilities  
- **Pronunciation Feedback Accuracy**: Technical limitations in speech recognition
- **Performance Under Load**: System degradation with multiple concurrent users

*Risk Assessment Matrix*:
[cols="3,2,2,2", options="header"]
|===
| Risk | Probability | Impact | Severity
| Authentication state inconsistencies | High | High | Critical
| Accessibility non-compliance | Medium | High | High
| Inaccurate pronunciation feedback | Medium | High | High
| Performance degradation | Low | Medium | Medium
| Data loss during synchronization | Low | High | Medium
|===

==== 1.9.2 Countermeasure Exploration and Evaluation

*Authentication Race Conditions*:
- **Countermeasure**: Implement TLA+ formal verification for all state transitions
- **Evaluation**: Formal methods provide mathematical guarantees; implementation cost high but justified for critical path
- **Result**: Requirements updated to mandate formal verification for authentication flows

*Accessibility Compliance Gaps*:
- **Countermeasure**: Implement WCAG 2.2 AA compliance from initial design phase
- **Evaluation**: Proactive compliance reduces rework; requires specialized expertise
- **Result**: Accessibility requirements integrated into all interface specifications

*Pronunciation Feedback Accuracy*:
- **Countermeasure**: Implement progressive feedback with confidence scoring
- **Evaluation**: Graceful degradation maintains usability when accuracy uncertain
- **Result**: Machine requirements updated to include confidence thresholds

==== 1.9.3 Risk-Driven Requirements Changes

The risk analysis resulted in the following requirement modifications:

*Added Requirements*:
- RQ-SEC-001: All authentication state transitions must be formally verified using TLA+
- RQ-ACC-001: All user interfaces must comply with WCAG 2.2 AA standards
- RQ-PER-001: System must maintain <2s response time under 1000 concurrent users

*Modified Requirements*:
- Updated authentication timeout from 5s to 3s based on performance risk assessment
- Enhanced error handling requirements to include accessibility considerations
- Strengthened data persistence requirements based on synchronization risk analysis

==== 2.1.3 Domain Narrative
*Independent of any specific software system, this narrative describes how pronunciation learning occurs in the real world*

Language learners like Maria and Carlos engage with English pronunciation as a physical skill requiring both auditory discrimination and muscular coordination. Maria, working in international marketing, discovers that certain English sounds don't exist in her phonetic repertoire - her mouth has never formed the /θ/ sound required for "three." She experiences this as both a cognitive challenge (hearing the difference) and a physical one (producing the sound).

Carlos, preparing to teach abroad, confronts the reality that English vowel sounds occur in different positions than Spanish vowels. His students' laughter at "beach" reveals that his auditory perception hasn't developed the sensitivity to distinguish similar English vowels.

The learning process unfolds through repeated cycles: learners attempt unfamiliar sounds, receive feedback about the accuracy, adjust their articulation, and gradually develop both the auditory discrimination and muscular control needed for clear pronunciation. Progress occurs incrementally, with plateaus and breakthroughs, influenced by consistent practice and quality feedback.

Technical systems intersect with this learning process when they provide feedback, track progress, or create barriers through unreliable performance. The ideal technological support would understand the physical nature of pronunciation learning and provide specific, actionable guidance without introducing additional frustrations.

=== 2.4 System Design Patterns and Algebraic Structures

==== 2.4.1 Domain-Driven Algebraic Design

The system employs algebraic structures that closely model domain phenomena, ensuring mathematical consistency while maintaining domain faithfulness. Key algebraic properties include:

*Closure*: All operations return values within the same type system
*Associativity*: Component compositions maintain predictable behavior
*Identity*: Neutral elements exist for state transitions and user interactions

==== 2.4.2 Design Pattern Applications

*Factory Pattern*: `LoadingStrategyFactory` creates appropriate loading strategies based on operation context
*Strategy Pattern*: Interchangeable loading animations (`PulsatingWaveStrategy`, `CircularProgressStrategy`)
*Observer Pattern*: `LoadingStateManager` notifies UI components of state changes
*Singleton Pattern*: `SessionController` ensures single authentication state instance
*Template Method*: Base loading class defines pronunciation fact display algorithm

==== 2.4.3 Algebraic Structures and Closure Under Operations

===== 2.4.3.1 Loading Strategy Creation

*Initial Approach*:
createLoadingStrategy(type: String): LoadingStrategy

*Closed Operation*:
createLoadingStrategy(context: OperationContext): LoadingStrategy


*Improvement*: The function evolved from taking a simple string type to accepting an `OperationContext` object that encapsulates all relevant parameters (operation type, duration, user preferences, error state). This closure allows the function to handle all loading strategy creation scenarios without modification, including error states, authentication flows, and data processing operations. The `OperationContext` can be extended with new properties without changing the function signature.

===== 2.4.3.2 User Authentication Flow

*Initial Approach*:
authenticateUser(email: String, password: String): Boolean


*Closed Operation*:
authenticateUser(credentials: AuthCredentials): AuthenticationResult


*Improvement*: The function now returns an `AuthenticationResult` that can represent success, failure, or partial states (like requiring additional verification). The `AuthCredentials` object can be extended to support multiple authentication methods (OAuth, biometrics, etc.) without modifying the core authentication logic. This closure ensures all possible authentication outcomes are handled within the same algebraic structure.

===== 2.4.3.3 Pronunciation Assessment

*Initial Approach*:
evaluatePronunciation(audio: AudioRecording, target: String): Float

*Closed Operation*:
evaluatePronunciation(attempt: PronunciationAttempt): FeedbackResult


*Improvement*: The function now takes a `PronunciationAttempt` that encapsulates audio data, metadata, and context, and returns a `FeedbackResult` that can contain multiple types of feedback (accuracy scores, specific error highlights, improvement suggestions). This closure allows the system to handle various assessment scenarios - from simple word pronunciation to complex sentence intonation - using the same algebraic structure, while providing room for future extensions like visual feedback or comparative analysis.

===== 2.4.3.4 Design Benefits

The application of closure under operations provides several key benefits:

*Extensibility*: Each operation can accommodate new scenarios without signature changes
*Composability*: Closed operations can be combined to form more complex behaviors
*Maintainability*: The algebraic structure ensures consistent error handling and state management
*Domain Faithfulness*: The operations better represent real-world language learning phenomena where outcomes are often complex and multi-faceted

These closed operations form an algebra that accurately models the pronunciation learning domain while providing a robust foundation for future feature development.

=== 2.6 Requirements Conflict Resolution Framework

This section implements a structured playbook for detecting, classifying, and resolving requirements conflicts to eliminate ambiguity early and protect downstream design and testing.

==== 2.6.1 Conflict Taxonomy and Classification

*Terminology Clash*:: Different words for same concept (e.g., "attempt" vs "try")
*Designation Clash*:: Same word for different concepts (e.g., "User" meaning student vs staff)
*Structure Clash*:: Same concept with different structure (e.g., point vs interval timing)
*Weak Conflict*:: Unsatisfiable under boundary conditions but resolvable by adjustment
*Strong Conflict*:: Intrinsically contradictory and cannot be simultaneously satisfied

==== 2.6.2 Resolution Workflow

The team follows a 5-step process for conflict resolution:

. *Find Overlaps*: Cluster statements touching same phenomena, actors, or data
. *Detect & Classify*: Identify clash type(s) and strength; document boundary conditions
. *Document*: Open Conflict Log entry with ID, statements, type, and boundary
. *Generate Candidates*: Apply resolution tactics to create ≥3 candidate solutions
. *Evaluate & Select*: Choose option preserving maximum value and NFRs
. *Propagate*: Update glossary/requirements; add verification hooks and link evidence

==== 2.6.3 Resolution Tactics

*Specialize*:: Split ambiguous terms (User → StudentUser/StaffUser)
*Restore*:: Clarify sequence and structure ("Submit within 5 minutes, then feedback for 15 minutes")
*Weaken*:: Add exception clauses ("unless accommodation applies")
*Avoid*:: Prevent boundary conditions through UI/UX design
*Drop*:: Remove redundant or low-value conflicting requirements

==== 2.6.4 Applied Examples

*Case: User Role Ambiguity*
* Conflict: "User can view quiz feedback" vs "User can manage grading rubrics"
* Resolution: Specialize → StudentUser (read-only feedback) vs StaffUser (rubric management)
* Verification: Role-based tests for permission boundaries

*Case: Timing Structure Conflict*  
* Conflict: "Submit within 5 minutes" (interval) vs "Feedback until 12:05" (point)
* Resolution: Restore → "Submit within 5 minutes; feedback available for 15 minutes after submission"
* Verification: Boundary tests at time limits
